# Limitations

What the system cannot do, and what has not been tested. Kept up to date as work progresses.

- The system has not been flown on a farm. All results come from public datasets and simulation.
- Detection and counting are trained and measured on one public dataset (ICAERUS, France: three farms for cross-validation, one held out for the final test). How well they transfer to farms, breeds and pastures elsewhere is not measured.
- Duplicate removal across images (S3) is measured on ICAERUS flights whose animal links were checked by hand. Ten flights are verified, at least one from each farm-date group, all checked by one person, and there is no published external benchmark to compare against.
- No Raspberry Pi 5 was available, so onboard herd-detection speed (S7) is an estimate, not a measurement.
- ICAERUS has no calf labels, so the pixels needed for calves are inferred from adult cattle and published calf sizes, not measured on calves.
- Some ICAERUS images labelled as empty contain cattle (at least two found). Scores use the published labels unchanged (D-32), so precision and false-detection rates are slightly understated.
- Derval has only one survey day, so in cross-validation it is scored by a model that never saw that farm at all. It is where the Phase 1 detector does worst (`results/baselines/dfine_failures.json`). This is the closest evidence available of how the detector may do on new farms, breeds and light.
- The herd-register alerts (S4) are measured only on simulated farms. The farm assumptions come from published sources where they exist (`configs/sim_farms.yaml`), and results are reported across the range of each one, but how much larger the count error is for closely packed herds (1.5 times, from 20 animals in a camp) is assumed, not measured, and it drives most of the remaining false alarms.
- A single dead animal is rarely detected by a drone count of a whole camp: about 1 in 5 single deaths is caught within 3 checks in simulation, mostly by chance. Alerts are designed for losses of several animals.
- How herds spread across a whole camp (number of groups, distances between them, preference for water and shade) is not in the public data, which was photographed where cattle were. The simulator sweeps it (`configs/sim_worlds.yaml`).
- Simulated detections of calves and of animals partly hidden by trees are assumptions: no labels of calves or hidden animals exist, so their detection is extrapolated from adults and swept.
- The flying height uses a provisional pixel budget for calves (the smallest labelled adults), until the Phase 2 resolution study measures it.
- On a farm it has never seen, the detector finds 55% of cattle, against 73% on farms it was trained on (leave-one-farm-out, `results/phase2/g6_lofo.json`). Two training changes aimed at this did not help (D-49, D-50). Training on labelled photos of each farm before use does help, but levels off at about 200 photos below the agreed 80% found and 80% correct (G13, D-52; two public farms). It still needs checking on real farms.
- The onboard herd detector does not fully meet its targets (S7). With stills reduced to 1,920 px it finds 99% of herds but groups 83% correctly, against 85% (cross-validation, D-51). The larger image doubles the work per still, and onboard speed is still an estimate.
- Returning home with the camera after GPS loss is not finished. In simulation it has landed within 0.3 m of take-off in calm air and in 5 m/s wind on several worlds, but not reliably: on one new world it fell back to a blind descent in calm air (11 m away), several 5 m/s flights landed accurately yet broke the position-error limits on the way, and it fails in 10 m/s. The camera is fixed to the body, and when the drone leans hard into strong wind it sees the ground at a slant and matching fails. It assumes a downward laser rangefinder (without GPS, the barometer's height drifted several metres), and fine texture on the ground near the landing point. A stabilised camera is the likely fix; none of this has been flown.
- The automated mission test (S5) flies one simulated survey per run on GitHub's machines. It shows the whole chain works (plan, fly, photograph, count at home base); it says nothing about real wind, light, radio links or airframes.
- The dashboard has been built and tested by its developer only until the five usability sessions (S6) are run with non-technical testers.
- The held-back test farm (Phase 7) has 62 photos from five flights and no empty photos, so its intervals are wide and it cannot measure false detections on empty ground.
- On the held-back test farm, scored once (Phase 7, D-54), the final system meets 1 of 9 targets: it finds 69% of cattle with 74% of marks correct (targets 80%), its per-photo count is off by 2.2 animals on photos holding about 22 each (target 0.45), and its 90% count range holds the true count on 79% of photos (target 85%). The count ranges were fitted on sparse photos and are too narrow for dense herds; ranges that widen with the count were tried on cross-validation and are too wide to be useful (D-55). Most misses are animals standing close together: 54% of them were found, against 82% of animals standing apart (`results/phase7/test_farm_failures.json`).
- On the same farm the onboard herd detector found 52% of herds and grouped 42% (targets 95% and 85%), against 99% and 83% in cross-validation, with the same flying height. Dense, touching herds on a farm it has not seen are its weak point, so onboard herd mode is not ready to steer a flight.
- Counting a whole flight with the final detector is off by 25% on average on the 20 hand-checked flights, against the 15% target (S3). With perfect detections the same method gets to 12.5%, so the detector is the limit, not the matching between photos.
- The alert target (S4) is not met with the final detector's count error: on 4,000 simulated farms held back for one test, the standard rule raised 1.6 false alarms per 100 surveys and caught 46% of losses of five or more, against at most 1.5 and at least 65% (D-56). The farms, losses and record errors are simulated.
