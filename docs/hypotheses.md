# Hypotheses

Written before each test is run. Each outcome is reported, whether the hypothesis held or not. An outcome is filled in only from a results file.

## Phase 1: baselines (written 2026-11-18, before any model was run)

All Phase 1 baselines are scored by grouped three-fold cross-validation over farm-date groups (D-26): every non-test image is predicted by a model that never saw its farm and day. The test farm (Other_farms) and the Shao et al. set are not touched until Phase 7.

*Edited 2026-11-18, before any model was run: "validation flights" and "calibration flights" became out-of-fold predictions when the split design changed (D-25, D-26). The predictions themselves are unchanged.*

| ID | Hypothesis | Prediction | How it is judged | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | A detector fine-tuned on the training farms beats an open-vocabulary detector with no cattle training. | Fine-tuned centre-matched AP at least 0.2 higher, and per-image count mean absolute error (MAE) at most half. | Centre AP (mean over 0.50:0.95) and count MAE on out-of-fold images (the zero-shot image sample), with bootstrap 95% intervals over farm-date groups; the intervals must not overlap | **Not held.** Centre AP 0.51 vs 0.25 (gain met), but count MAE 0.77 vs 0.95 (not halved), and both pairs of intervals overlap (133 images) |
| H2 | Count errors grow with herd density. | Per-image MAE divided by the true count is at least twice as large for images with 20 or more animals as for images with 1 to 4. | Relative MAE per band of true count, with bootstrap intervals over farm-date groups | **Not held (reversed).** Relative MAE 0.12 for 20+ animals vs 0.42 for 1–4 |
| H3 | Conformal count intervals fitted on other folds hold on the held-out fold. | A nominal 90% interval contains the true count on 85% to 95% of out-of-fold images. | Coverage on out-of-fold images; also reported per herd-size band | **Held.** Coverage 0.93 (0.87–0.98); lowest on images with 20+ animals (0.78) |
| H4 | Detection is harder from 100 m than from 60 m. | Recall at the operating point at least 0.05 lower at 100 m. | Recall per altitude group, from the EXIF relative altitude, with bootstrap intervals over farm-date groups | **Not held (reversed).** Recall 0.75 at 60 m vs 0.88 at 100 m; the 60 m gap comes from the Derval farm, not height (failure review) |
| H5 | Empty pasture rarely produces false detections after fine-tuning. | Fewer than 0.05 false detections per image on out-of-fold images with no cattle. | False positives per empty out-of-fold image at the operating point | **Not held.** 0.33 false detections per empty image (0.07–0.73), mostly farmyard objects |
| H6 | Adding up per-image counts overcounts a flight, because overlapping images see the same animals. | The naive sum is more than 20% above the true number of animals on every flight tested. | Per-flight count against hand-linked identities (only if overlapping, geotagged flights exist; otherwise reported as not tested) | **Not held, narrowly.** Smallest overcount 17% over 10 verified flights; missed animals partly offset the double counts (with perfect detection every flight is above 20%) |

The operating point (score threshold) for each fold is the one that minimises count MAE on the other folds' out-of-fold predictions, fixed before that fold is scored.

## Phase 3: counting across images (written 2026-11-22, after the error split and before any Phase 3 method was built)

Scored on the 20 owner-verified flights (`results/data/s3_truth.json`), every learned part fitted on the other two folds, and on 200 simulated validation flights. The thresholds are also recorded in `configs/phase3.yaml`, frozen before the first run that tests any of them. Baseline: the Phase 1 method (registration, then greedy grouping; D-27, D-30), mean absolute relative flight error 0.145 with labels as detections and 0.285 with the Phase 1 detector (`results/phase3/error_split.json`). Real flights are public data, not field data; simulated flights are simulation results.

| ID | Hypothesis | Prediction | How it is judged | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H23 | Grouping all detections of a flight at once (correlation clustering) beats greedy joining. | Mean absolute relative flight error lower by at least 0.05 on the verified flights, with the paired 95% interval excluding zero, and not worse on simulated validation flights (upper end of the paired interval at most +0.01). | The same detections and pair evidence, global against greedy grouping, paired over flights | **Not held** (real part). With the Phase 1 detector's detections and the fixed-gate evidence (chosen before the run), mean error 0.270 with global grouping and 0.265 with greedy joining: a gain of -0.005 (-0.074 to 0.056) against the 0.05 required (`results/phase3/grouping_real.json`). The simulated part cannot change the verdict. With labels as detections (the linking alone), global grouping gives 0.091 against 0.125 greedy (difference -0.034, -0.094 to 0.016) and 0.145 for the Phase 1 method: the new evidence and global grouping help the linking, but with the Phase 1 detector the detection errors dominate. Simulated part (200 validation flights, `results/phase3/sim_validation.json`): global grouping is worse by 0.013 (0.008 to 0.018), beyond the 0.01 margin |
| H24 | Allowing for how far an animal can walk between two stills helps where the same ground is photographed on separate passes. | On flights with overlapping stills more than 2 minutes apart, the error is lower than with a fixed distance gate, on verified and simulated flights, with the simulated paired interval excluding zero. | Movement allowance against the Phase 1 fixed gate, paired over those flights; the real subset is small and reported as direction only | **Not held.** No real flight has overlapping stills more than 2 minutes apart. On the 136 of 200 simulated validation flights that do, the movement allowance changes the error by +0.002 (-0.001 to 0.005) against the fixed gate (`results/phase3/sim_validation.json`). In simulation, as in the real pair data, grazing over a few minutes moves animals much less than the gate already allows |
| H25 | Appearance helps decide whether two detections are one animal. | Out-of-fold area under the ROC curve of the same-animal model at least 0.03 higher with appearance than with position, movement and size alone, the paired interval excluding zero. | Both named descriptors (`configs/phase3.yaml`) on the same out-of-fold pairs; appearance is kept only if one of them holds | **Not held.** On the Phase 1 detector's pairs, the out-of-fold AUC without appearance is 0.962; adding the colour-and-texture distance gives 0.955 (gain -0.007, -0.015 to 0.002) and the DINOv2 distance 0.964 (gain +0.002, 0.000 to 0.003). Alone, either distance separates pairs only weakly (AUC 0.71 and 0.72): at about 2 cm per pixel, cattle of one herd look alike (`results/phase3/h25.json`). Appearance is not used |
| H26 | Flight-count ranges are calibrated. | 90% ranges contain the true count on 85-95% of simulated validation flights; real-flight coverage reported beside it. | Coverage at 68, 90 and 95% on simulated and (indicative, 20 flights) real flights | **Not held: the ranges are too wide.** The counting method's 90% ranges, calibrated on the 20 real flights, contain the true count on 97.5% of simulated validation flights (68%: 77.5%; 95%: 99.5%), above the 85-95% band; real-flight coverage out of fold is 75%, 95% and 100% (indicative, 20 flights). With 20 calibration flights the 90% range is set by the two worst detection failures, so it is wide (`results/phase3/sim_validation.json`, `results/phase3/ranges_real.json`) |

## Phase 4: herd register and alerts (written 2026-11-21, before any alert rule was run)

Scored on simulated farms (`configs/sim_farms.yaml`, validation seed), never on field data. "Losses" are deaths and thefts; a loss is caught if an alert fires on its camp within 3 surveys. Each prediction is judged by a rule fixed in code before the run, at the 90% interval level unless stated, under the count error model measured in Phase 1.

| ID | Hypothesis | Prediction | How it is judged | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H13 | Alerting only when the whole interval is below the expected count removes most false alarms. | The interval rule has at least 5 times fewer false alarms per 100 surveys than the naive rule, and catches no more than 10 percentage points fewer losses of 5 or more animals. | False alarms per 100 surveys and share of losses of 5 or more caught, naive against interval rule | **Not held.** False alarms fell from 19.68 to 3.75 per 100 surveys (more than 5 times fewer), but the share of losses of 5 or more caught fell from 86% to 50%, more than the 10 points allowed |
| H14 | A recount before alerting removes most of the remaining false alarms at little cost in speed. | The recount rule has at most half the interval rule's false alarms per 100 surveys, and its mean surveys until a loss of 5 or more is caught is no more than 1 higher. | Same measures, interval against recount rule | **Held.** False alarms 3.75 → 1.51 per 100 surveys; mean surveys until caught 1.55 → 1.60 |
| H15 | Unrecorded moves cause most false alarms that survive the recount, and the farm-level check removes at least half of those. | The farm rule has at most half the recount rule's false alarms per 100 surveys on farms with unrecorded moves. | False alarms per 100 surveys, recount against farm rule, on farms whose unrecorded-move share is above 0.05 | **Not held.** On farms with unrecorded moves, false alarms 1.41 → 1.33 per 100 surveys; the farm-level check rarely finds a matching surplus |

### Phase 4, second round (written 2026-11-21, after the S4 breakdowns, before the change was run)

The breakdowns (`results/phase4/s4_breakdown_validation.json`) showed that most false alarms left after the recount rule come from count error in camps of 20 or more animals, and that most of these are raised by the large-loss shortcut (without it, false alarms fall from 1.45 to 0.51 per 100 surveys) (shortfall of at least 10 animals below the 90% interval, alerted without a recount), not after a recount. The change: skip the recount only when the 99% interval, not the 90% one, is at least 10 animals below the expected count. Judged as H13-H15 are, with the 4000 validation farms.

| ID | Hypothesis | Prediction | How it is judged | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H16 | Most false alarms left after the recount come from the large-loss shortcut firing on count error in large camps; a stricter shortcut removes them at little cost. | With the stricter shortcut, the recount rule at 90% has at least 40% fewer false alarms per 100 surveys, and catches no more than 3 percentage points fewer losses of 5 or more. | False alarms per 100 surveys and share of losses of 5 or more caught, recount rule at 90%, current against stricter shortcut | **Not held.** False alarms fell by 57%, from 1.45 to 0.62 per 100 surveys, but the share of losses of 5 or more caught fell from 58% to 43%: the shortcut also catches most large thefts at once, so making it stricter loses them |
| H17 | With fewer false alarms, a lower dial setting meets the S4 target. | The recount rule with the stricter shortcut meets the frozen S4 target (configs/specs.yaml `s4_alert_targets`) at one or more interval levels on the validation farms. | Both S4 target measures at each interval level, recount rule with the stricter shortcut | **Not held.** Best points: 1.16 false alarms per 100 surveys with 54% caught (68% level), 0.95 with 51% (80%); none reaches 65% caught. The stricter shortcut moves along the trade-off curve rather than improving it |

### Phase 4, third round (written 2026-11-22, before the history rule was run)

The Phase 4 plan keeps a rule that uses each camp's survey history (a Kalman-type filter) as a stretch candidate if rules 1-4 leave a clear gap to the S4 target; they do (58% of losses of 5 or more caught against 65%). The **history rule** is the recount rule with one change: the decision uses a one-dimensional Kalman filter of the camp's shortfall (expected count minus true count) built from every survey since the camp's records were last confirmed, instead of the latest survey alone. Settings fixed before the run: each survey's error standard deviation is the half-width of its 68% interval; before each survey is used, the shortfall's variance grows by a fifth of that survey's error variance (for losses and unrecorded changes), so the filter weighs about the last three surveys whatever the herd size; after a correction the shortfall is known to within half an animal; a shortfall is flagged when the filter's lower bound at the dial level is above half an animal. Judged on the 4000 validation farms with the Phase 1 count error and independent surveys, as before.

| ID | Hypothesis | Prediction | How it is judged | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H18 | Using every survey since the records were last confirmed catches losses that a single survey misses, without more false alarms. | At one or more interval levels, the history rule has at most 1.5 false alarms per 100 surveys and catches at least 5 percentage points more losses of 5 or more than the recount rule at 90% (58%). | False alarms per 100 surveys and share of losses of 5 or more caught, history rule at each level against the recount rule at 90% | **Not held.** Under 1.5 false alarms per 100 surveys the history rule catches no more than the recount rule: 1.33 and 57% at 90%, 0.89 and 53% at 95% (recount at 90%: 58%). Its gain is at the low-false-alarm end: at 95% it catches 53% against the recount rule's 41% at 95% (0.61 false alarms) |
| H19 | With history, the S4 target can be met with today's count error. | The history rule meets the frozen S4 target at one or more interval levels. | Both S4 target measures at each interval level | **Not held** with the Phase 1 count error. With the S3 target's count error it meets the target at 90%: 1.49 false alarms per 100 surveys, 73% caught |

## Phase 5: simulation and the mission (written 2026-11-22 in the Phase 5 plan, before any simulated flight was run)

Scored on simulated camps (`configs/sim_worlds.yaml`, validation seed), never on field data. Detections, geotags and registration on simulated flights come from error models fitted to real flights (`results/data/`); simulated counts are simulation results.

| ID | Hypothesis | Prediction | How it is judged | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H20 | Animal movement during a survey raises the error of the flight count after duplicate removal. | With the measured movement and a moderate reaction to the drone, the mean absolute relative flight error is at least 1.5 times that with still animals. | Mean of abs(count - true) / true over 60 simulated flights, the same camps flown with still and with moving animals (`scripts/run_sim_flights.py`, `results/phase5/h20.json`) | **Not held.** Mean error 0.28 with still animals and 0.30 with movement, a ratio of 1.08 (1.03-1.18). No animal reacted: the planned height (83 m) is above every reaction height in the swept range |

| H22 | The simulation is consistent with real flights. | With the real-flight calibration, simulated 90% interval coverage of the flight count is within 10 percentage points of the real-flight coverage measured in Phase 3. | Coverage on simulated flights against Phase 3's real-flight coverage | **Held.** With the real-flight calibration, simulated 90% coverage is 97.5% against 95% real-flight coverage out of fold (Phase 3), within 10 percentage points.
